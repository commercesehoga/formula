The Ideal Gas Law formula

PV = nRT
  • Ppressure of the gas
  • Vvolume of the gas
  • namount of gas, in moles
  • Runiversal gas constant
  • Tabsolute temperature, in kelvin

The story

Think of a sealed flask as a classroom of gas particles. More students (n) or a hotter, more excited room (T) means harder bumping into the walls, so the pressure P rises. Give them a bigger room V and the bumping eases.

Real-life analogy

Think of a bicycle tyre. Pump in more air (n) and the pressure P rises. Leave it in the hot sun (T) and it gets harder. The tyre's fixed size is V.

Memory trick

Say the letters in order: P V n R T, or use the line 'Please Visit Nandu's Restaurant Today'.

Exam tip

Temperature must be in kelvin (K = °C + 273). Pick the value of R to match your units: 0.0821 L·atm/(mol·K) or 8.314 J/(mol·K).

FAQ

Questions about Ideal Gas Law

What is the Ideal Gas Law formula?
PV = nRT. For an ideal gas, pressure times volume equals the number of moles times the gas constant times the absolute temperature.
